# Open Questions

**Purpose:** Working tracker of every factual gap, ambiguity, and follow-up that has been surfaced during research. Questions are grouped by who can answer them. Each question carries an ID, a one-line note on why it matters, and a status field. As answers come in, mark resolved with the date and a one-line note on where the answer landed (which file, which entry).

**How to update:**
1. When an answer comes in, change the status from `Open` to `Resolved YYYY-MM-DD`, write a one-line answer under the question, and add a "Lands in:" pointer.
2. Optionally move resolved entries to the "Resolved questions" section at the bottom to keep the live list short.
3. New questions get a new ID under the right section and append-only to the existing numbering.

### T-02 — Graduated Rate Estate designation on first T3
- **Question:** Confirm the tax accountant will designate Mary's estate as a Graduated Rate Estate (GRE) on its first T3 return.
- **Why it matters:** Without GRE status, the estate is taxed at the top marginal rate (~46% combined federal + Ontario) on every dollar of post-death income from year one. With GRE status, the estate gets the same graduated personal-income-tax rates an individual would — substantial saving on any meaningful post-death income, which in Scenario B (*Pecore* upheld) could be considerable. The designation is a tick-box on the first T3 (per *Income Tax Act* s. 248(1) condition (d)) and is the single largest tax-planning decision available to Daughter as legal representative.
- **Conditions for GRE (all must be met):** (a) within 36 months of death; (b) is a testamentary trust; (c) Mary's SIN on the return; (d) the estate designates itself; (e) no other estate makes the designation. All five satisfied for Mary's situation.
- **Status:** Open
- **Answer:**
- **Lands in:** `findings/t3-estate-returns.md` Entry 2
