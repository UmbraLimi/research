# T3 Estate Trust Returns — Mary's Estate as a Separate Taxpayer

**Topic:** What the T3 trust return is, when it applies to Mary's estate, the Graduated Rate Estate (GRE) election, year-end choice, and filing deadlines.

**Why this matters for Daughter (Q2, Q3, Q5, Q6):**

- When Mary died on 2026-04-21, her estate became a **separate taxpayer**. Income earned by the estate from that moment forward is taxed on a T3 return filed by the estate (with Daughter as legal representative), not on Mary's terminal T1.
- The T3 stream runs in parallel with the terminal T1 — both are tax filings, but for different time periods and different "taxpayers."
- The GRE election is the single most consequential tax-planning decision available to Daughter as legal representative. Skipping it (or losing eligibility) means the estate pays tax at the **top marginal rate** on every dollar of income from year one. Making it correctly means the estate pays at **graduated personal rates** for up to 36 months — potentially saving thousands.

**Cross-reference:** Income earned by Mary up to and including the moment of death is on Mary's 2026 terminal T1 (`findings/deemed-disposition.md`). Income earned by the estate *after* death is on the T3, covered here. The split point is 2026-04-21.

## Entry 1 — What the T3 estate trust return is

**Captured:** 2026-05-14
**Source:** *Income Tax Act* and CRA practice.

### The basic mechanic

When Mary died on 2026-04-21:

1. Mary's tax year ended that moment. Her income up to that date goes on the **terminal T1** for 2026 (due 2027-04-30).
2. Mary's estate came into existence the same moment. The estate is treated by the *Income Tax Act* as a **testamentary trust** — a separate taxpayer from Mary, with its own tax year, its own returns, and its own tax bill.
3. Income earned by the estate's assets between 2026-04-21 and the wind-up of the estate goes on the estate's **T3 trust return**.

### What "earned by the estate" means

Any income the estate's assets generate after death:

- **Interest** on bank balances during administration
- **Dividends** paid on shares the estate holds
- **Distributions** from mutual funds held by the estate
- **Capital gains** realized when the estate sells assets (e.g., to facilitate distribution to beneficiaries)
- **Trust distributions** from any trust where Mary or her estate was a beneficiary

For Mary's situation, the practical sources of post-death income are mostly:
- Interest on the chequing and savings balances while they sit
- Dividends, interest, or distributions on the non-registered investments (until sold or distributed)
- Any capital gain (or loss) when investments are sold for distribution purposes — measured against the **stepped-up cost base** from the s. 70(5) deemed disposition (i.e., from FMV on 2026-04-21 onward)

### What's NOT on the T3

- Income earned by Mary up to the moment of death — that's terminal T1.
- The deemed-disposition capital gain on the date of death itself — terminal T1.
- Mary's RIF inclusion under s. 146.3(6) — terminal T1.
- Income on assets that passed outside the estate (e.g., the RIF to Daughter as named beneficiary; joint assets *if* the *Pecore* analysis lands on gift-of-survivorship and they truly passed to Daughter at death) — taxed on the recipient's personal T1, not on the estate's T3.

## Entry 2 — The Graduated Rate Estate (GRE) — the most important election

**Captured:** 2026-05-14
**Source:** *Income Tax Act*, R.S.C. 1985, c. 1 (5th Supp.), s. 248(1) "graduated rate estate" definition.
**URL:** https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-248.html
**Source quality:** Primary (consolidated federal statute).

### Why this matters (in one sentence)

Without GRE status, the estate pays tax at the **top federal marginal rate (33%)** plus Ontario's top marginal rate (~13.16%) on every dollar of income from the first dollar. With GRE status, the estate pays at the same **graduated personal-income tax rates** an individual would — meaning the first ~$55,000 is taxed at the lowest federal bracket (15%) and graduated brackets apply thereafter. On a modest estate, this can mean the difference between paying ~46% federal+Ontario combined and paying ~20% combined.

### Verbatim definition (s. 248(1))

> "**graduated rate estate**, of an individual at any time, means the estate that arose on and as a consequence of the individual's death if
> (a) that time is no more than 36 months after the death,
> (b) the estate is at that time a testamentary trust,
> (c) the individual's Social Insurance Number (or if the individual had not, before the death, been assigned a Social Insurance Number, such other information as is acceptable to the Minister) is provided in the estate's return of income under Part I for the taxation year that includes that time and for each of its earlier taxation years that ended after 2015,
> (d) the estate designates itself as the graduated rate estate of the individual in its return of income under Part I for its first taxation year that ends after 2015, and
> (e) no other estate designates itself as the graduated rate estate of the individual in a return of income under Part I for a taxation year that ends after 2015."

### How each condition applies to Mary's estate

- **(a) "no more than 36 months after the death"** — Mary died 2026-04-21. GRE status is available until **2029-04-21** at the latest. After that, the estate (if still in existence) ceases to be a GRE.
- **(b) "the estate is at that time a testamentary trust"** — Mary's estate is a testamentary trust because it arose on Mary's death under her will. ✓
- **(c) Mary's SIN must be on the T3 return** — administrative; Daughter will need Mary's SIN (which she'll have for the terminal T1 anyway).
- **(d) the estate must designate itself as GRE on its first T3** — this is the actual election. It is made *by checking the GRE box on the first T3 return*. Missing this is the single most common way GRE status is lost. The tax accountant will know to make the designation; flagging it here is belt-and-suspenders.
- **(e) only one GRE per deceased** — applies if the deceased's affairs are administered by multiple estates. Not applicable to Mary (single estate).

### What GRE status gives you, beyond graduated rates

1. **Choice of non-calendar fiscal year-end.** A regular trust must use a December 31 year-end. A GRE can pick any first-year-end up to 12 months from death. For Mary, the latest possible first-year-end is **2027-04-20** (one day shy of the first anniversary).
2. **Tax payment is on the same 90-day cycle as the filing deadline.** Non-GRE testamentary trusts also get this exemption from the quarterly-instalment rule, but only as a transitional rule that ended in 2016 — so in practice today the instalment exemption is GRE-only.
3. **Eligibility for certain loss-carryback elections** (notably s. 164(6), see Entry 6 below) that can apply the estate's first-year capital losses against gains on Mary's terminal T1.
4. **Donation flexibility** under amendments to s. 118.1 for charitable gifts made by will (not relevant here unless Mary's will makes a charitable bequest).

### Caution: GRE status is **for at most 36 months**

After 2029-04-21 (or whenever the estate is wound up earlier), the estate stops being a GRE. If the estate continues to exist beyond 36 months for any reason (e.g., ongoing litigation, an unsettled *Pecore* dispute, undisposed asset), it reverts to being a regular testamentary trust taxed at the top marginal rate from that point forward. **This is a strong incentive to wind up the estate within 36 months wherever possible.**

For Mary's estate: 36 months is more time than is needed for a normal administration. But the *Pecore* uncertainty could in theory drag past 36 months if a dispute with Brother became litigation. That risk argues for resolving the *Pecore* question quickly with the lawyer and not letting it fester.

## Entry 3 — Year-end choice strategy

**Captured:** 2026-05-14
**Source:** Practitioner guidance; CRA T3 Trust Guide (T4013).

The executor chooses the estate's first year-end. The choice has tax consequences and is one of the more interesting planning levers.

### The two common options

**Option A — Calendar year-end (December 31, 2026):**
- First T3 covers 2026-04-21 to 2026-12-31 (about 8.5 months).
- T3 filing deadline: 2027-03-31 (90 days after year-end).
- Second T3 covers 2027-01-01 to 2027-12-31.
- Simpler bookkeeping; matches Brother and Daughter's personal calendar tax years.

**Option B — Anniversary year-end (2027-04-20):**
- First T3 covers 2026-04-21 to 2027-04-20 (a full year).
- T3 filing deadline: 2027-07-19 (90 days after year-end).
- More room to spread income across the first year's graduated brackets.
- Cleaner conceptually (one return = one year of administration).

### Which is better for Mary?

It depends on the magnitude of post-death income, which itself depends on the *Pecore* outcome:

- If *Pecore* lands on resulting trust and the joint investments are estate property, the post-death income is likely meaningful (dividends + interest on ~however many hundred thousand $ of investments). Spreading it across more income via Option B's full year can use more graduated-bracket room.
- If *Pecore* is rebutted and the joint assets pass to Daughter outside the estate, the estate's post-death income is mostly just chequing/savings interest — small. Option A's simpler bookkeeping is likely fine.

This is a decision for the tax accountant to make once asset values and the *Pecore* analysis are settled.

### Constraint

The first taxation year of an estate cannot exceed 12 months under s. 249. So Option B's anniversary year-end (2027-04-20) is the latest possible first year-end for Mary's estate.

## Entry 4 — Filing deadline: 90 days after year-end

**Captured:** 2026-05-14
**Source:** *Income Tax Act* s. 150(1)(c).
**URL:** https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-150.html

### Verbatim text

> "(c) in the case of an estate or trust, within 90 days from the end of the year;"
> — ITA s. 150(1)(c).

### Application to Mary's estate

For the first T3:

| Year-end chosen | T3 filing deadline | Tax balance due |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-12-31 (calendar) | 2027-03-31 | 2027-03-31 |
| 2027-04-20 (anniversary) | 2027-07-19 | 2027-07-19 |
| Any earlier wind-up date (if estate is fully distributed earlier) | 90 days after wind-up | 90 days after wind-up |

Annual T3s continue every year (90 days after each subsequent year-end) until the estate is wound up. For Mary's estate, the realistic wind-up is sometime between mid-2027 and mid-2028 — by then the terminal T1 is assessed, the clearance certificate is in hand, and the residue can be distributed. So Mary's estate likely files **one or two T3s in total** before wind-up.

## Entry 5 — What income goes on Mary's T3 — the two Pecore scenarios

**Captured:** 2026-05-14
**Source:** Synthesis of `findings/jtwros-treatment.md` and the T3 framework above.

### Scenario A — *Pecore* presumption rebutted (joint assets pass to Daughter by survivorship)

The estate's assets are essentially:
- The $805 RIF? No — that passes to Daughter as named beneficiary outside the estate.
- The chequing/savings balances? Possibly — depends on the bank's decision about probate and the *Pecore* treatment of joint accounts specifically.
- Jewellery (specified bequests under the will) — not income-producing.
- No real property.
- No business.

In this scenario, the estate's post-death income is likely minimal — perhaps just interest on cash balances during the administration period. The T3 filings are mechanical and the tax is likely small.

### Scenario B — *Pecore* presumption upheld (resulting trust; joint assets fall into estate)

The estate holds:
- All the joint chequing/savings balances (beneficially).
- All the non-registered investments (beneficially).
- Jewellery.
- Plus the $805 RIF passes to Daughter outside the estate; that remains true regardless of *Pecore*.

In this scenario, the estate's post-death income could be significant. Dividends, interest, and any capital gain or loss from selling investments for distribution all flow through the T3. The GRE election becomes much more valuable. The year-end choice becomes a more interesting tax-planning decision.

### Practical note

The tax accountant will need to coordinate with the financial advisor / brokerage to get monthly statements showing the income earned by each asset from 2026-04-21 onward. The brokerage typically issues T3 / T5 slips to the *estate's* trust account number (see Entry 6 below) rather than to Mary's SIN, once notified of the death.

## Entry 6 — Practical setup: getting a trust account number, the s. 164(6) loss carryback

**Captured:** 2026-05-14
**Source:** CRA T3 Trust Guide and ITA s. 164(6).

### Trust account number

Before the estate can file a T3 (or have income slips issued to it), it needs a **CRA trust account number** (format: T followed by 8 digits). The legal representative applies using Form **T3APP** (Application for a Trust Account Number). The number is what brokerages and banks will need to issue T3/T5 slips in the estate's name.

The tax accountant can handle this. Should be done early in the administration — at the same time the financial institutions are being notified of the death.

### Section 164(6) loss carryback election

**The opportunity:** If the estate sells investments in its first year and realizes a capital loss (e.g., because the FMV at sale is below the stepped-up cost base from the date of death), s. 164(6) lets the legal representative *elect* to apply that estate-year capital loss back against Mary's terminal-T1 capital gains.

**Why it matters:** Without this election, the estate's capital loss is trapped in the estate (carried back against future estate gains, but maybe never used if the estate doesn't have future gains because it's about to wind up). With the election, the loss directly reduces Mary's terminal-T1 tax bill — a real cash saving for the estate.

**Practical relevance to Mary:** if the investments are sold during administration to facilitate distribution to Daughter and Brother, and the market has moved against them post-death, the s. 164(6) election may be available. Worth flagging for the tax accountant.

**Election deadline:** must be filed in the trust's return for its first year, and must be made jointly with an amendment to the deceased's terminal-T1 return.

## Entry 8 — Answers to Daughter's questions

**Q3 — When will taxes be due?** (T3 piece, adding to the answer in `findings/deemed-disposition.md`)

Beyond Mary's 2026 terminal T1 (due 2027-04-30):

- **Estate's first T3**: 90 days after the chosen first year-end. If calendar year-end → 2027-03-31. If anniversary year-end → 2027-07-19.
- **Subsequent T3s**: annually, 90 days after each year-end, until the estate is wound up.
- **Final T3**: 90 days after the wind-up date.

**Q5 — What is taxed?** (T3 piece, adding to the EAT and terminal-T1 answers)

Income earned by the estate's assets between death and wind-up — chiefly interest, dividends, distributions, and any capital gains realized on sale (measured against the stepped-up cost base from the date of death). Not the principal value of the assets themselves; just the income they generate during administration.

**Q6 — Rough sequence** (T3 piece — adds to the timeline in `findings/distribution-timing.md`)

The T3 obligations run in parallel with the terminal T1, not after. So the sequence has two simultaneous tracks:

- Track A (Mary's personal tax): terminal T1 due 2027-04-30, then Notice of Assessment, then TX19 clearance certificate request.
- Track B (estate's tax): T3 trust account number applied for early in administration; first T3 due in 2027; further T3s as needed; final T3 90 days after wind-up.

Both tracks need to be complete before the CRA clearance certificate can be issued (and therefore before fully-safe distribution). The two tracks reinforce why the realistic full-distribution date is **late 2027 to mid-2028**.

## Action items arising from this findings file

1. **Tax accountant: apply for a CRA trust account number for the estate** (Form T3APP) early in administration. Without this number, banks and brokerages can't issue T3/T5 slips to the estate.
2. **Tax accountant: confirm intention to designate GRE status on the first T3 return.** This is a tick-box on the first T3 but missing it means losing graduated rates from day one.

5. **Watch for s. 164(6) election opportunities.** If investments are sold during the estate's first year at a loss (relative to their FMV at the date of death), the election to carry the loss back to Mary's terminal T1 is potentially valuable.
6. **Plan wind-up within 36 months.** GRE status expires automatically at 2029-04-21. Beyond that, the estate (if still alive) pays at the top marginal rate. The *Pecore* uncertainty is the biggest risk to a clean wind-up timeline — another reason to resolve it quickly.
